## Reader decision and evidence boundary

- **C19.1 (verified):** The reader decision is whether each relation in an in-process transformed-sequence chain—operand interpretation, dependence capture, order selection, validation, live-value construction, interference, capacity, placement, spill/fill rewriting, and output closure—is strong enough to authorize the result. A final `valid=true` cannot repair an unsound earlier relation.
- **C19.2 (qualified):** Scheduler and liveness are executable standalone static transforms: both are members of `libtucmodel.a`, have Makefile rules, are aggregate `make test` prerequisites, and reproduce 14/14 and 12/12 focused results in a disposable exact-pin archive. Linkage and focused success do not prove semantic equivalence or production reachability.
- **C19.3 (rejected):** The pin does not establish an ONNX/ASM→scheduler→liveness→binary/queue/runtime pipeline. Exact header-derived inventories find no external non-test caller for either pass, neither implementation calls the other, shipped JSON/YAML exposes no controls for them, and documentation snippets are not repository call paths.
- **C19.4 (verified):** Scheduler and allocator both consume and produce arrays of the ABI-local packed `tu_instruction_t`. Shared representation establishes adjacency only; it does not establish identical operand interpretation, pass ordering, portable encoding, runtime dispatch, or composition.

## Scheduler public surface and build/config reachability

- **C19.5 (verified):** `tu_scheduler.h` declares exactly nine public `tu_sched_*` APIs: access analysis, DAG construction, mobility, DMA-hoist analysis, barrier-insertion analysis, full run, validation, result printing, and graph printing. All are defined in `tu_scheduler.c`; no external non-test caller exists outside implementation/header, tests, and docs.
- **C19.6 (qualified):** `tu_sched_config_t` is direct-C-API configuration. Policy, `hoist_dma`, `insert_barriers`, and `max_hoist_distance` have implementation consumers; `pipeline_tiles`, `max_window`, and `verbose` are declared/defaulted but inert in `tu_scheduler.c`. No shipped JSON/YAML or global runtime-conversion path constructs this config.
- **C19.7 (qualified):** Passing `NULL` and explicitly passing `tu_sched_config_default` take different internal paths: only the explicit object invokes hoist/barrier reporting. At this pin those helpers only return counts and list scheduling resets both counts; the focused probe finds byte-identical emitted orders and zero retained counts. No final-result behavioral difference is established.

## Scheduler dependence and order semantics

- **C19.8 (qualified):** Scheduler access extraction is an opcode-specific approximation over W/A/O byte ranges. DMA classes derive region and range from flags/dimensions; selected compute classes use opcode-specific fields, fixed 64-KiB spans, or full-region sentinels; unknown/control/layout operations carry no SRAM relation. It is not a complete memory-effect model for every named ISA opcode.
- **C19.9 (qualified):** DAG construction checks pairwise prior RAW, WAR, and WAW overlap and gives a barrier up to sixteen prior non-barrier predecessors. The finite predecessor and successor arrays silently skip additional intended edges rather than rejecting construction, so `built=true` proves only the retained graph.
- **C19.10 (rejected):** A scheduler barrier is not a complete before/after ordering point. The barrier depends on retained prior non-barrier nodes, but ordinary later nodes are not made successors merely by position. The focused probe reproduces both post-`BARRIER` and post-`HALT` DMA crossing under BALANCED policy. Chapter 11’s command-queue barrier remains a separate runtime contract.
- **C19.11 (qualified):** Mobility uses fixed one-cycle DMA and four-cycle non-DMA predecessor costs. These values are source-local analytical constants, not queue ticks, engine service, calibrated hardware latency, or physical overlap.
- **C19.12 (rejected):** `tu_sched_hoist_dma()` does not move graph nodes or change priority metadata. It counts selected candidates. `tu_sched_insert_barriers()` counts selected DMA-store successor patterns but emits no `SYNC` or `BARRIER` instruction. Their names and positive counts do not establish transformations.
- **C19.13 (qualified):** Full list scheduling can reorder ready nodes according to ASAP, ALAP, or BALANCED priority and original-ID tie breaking. The shipped five-topology sweep reproduces identical cycle/count/length rows for all policies, including pipeline-tiles `28/0/0/13`; it does not report exact order or prove the policies equivalent on all graphs.
- **C19.14 (verified):** Full scheduling resets result count fields inside `list_schedule()`, after direct hoist/barrier analyses may have written them. Therefore `tu_sched_run()` can erase positive `num_dma_hoisted` and `num_barriers_inserted` observations even when the named analyses counted candidates.
- **C19.15 (qualified):** `estimated_cycles` is the serial emission sum: one per DMA-class node and four per every other emitted node. It is independent of overlap and is not a DAG critical-path result despite the historical scheduler-sweep explanation.
- **C19.16 (rejected):** `tu_sched_validate()` is not a fail-closed transformed-sequence proof. It maps output instructions to original nodes using opcode, `dim0`, `dim1`, and flags while omitting `dim2` and immediates; duplicate/near-duplicate identities can be misassigned, and unmatched original nodes are skipped. Exact adversarial duplicates and missing mappings must be mutation-gated.
- **C19.17 (qualified):** `list_schedule()` sets `valid` only when emitted count equals graph node count, but that equality is relative to a graph whose intended edges may already have truncated. Schedule validity and intended-dependence completeness are separate gates.

## Liveness public surface and value construction

- **C19.18 (verified):** `tu_liveness.h` declares exactly seven public `tu_live_*` APIs: analysis, interference construction, coloring, apply, full allocation, result printing, and interference printing. All are defined in `tu_liveness.c`; no external non-test caller exists outside implementation/header, tests, and docs.
- **C19.19 (rejected):** Liveness use binding is not byte-range aware. `find_or_create_vreg()` explicitly discards use `start/end` and extends the most recently defined VReg in the same W/A/O region. A use can therefore bind to a newer disjoint value rather than the definition covering its original virtual range.
- **C19.20 (qualified):** Every recognized write creates a new VReg; a use with no prior definition creates an implicit VReg with `first_def=-1`, but later uses skip such implicit records and can create another one. The use classifier’s unbounded `opcode >= TU_ISA_LAYER_NORM` condition also misclassifies later numeric opcode families—including observed DMA loads—as A-region uses. Scheduler and liveness extraction are therefore not identical, and a live interval is meaningful only after opcode and value identity are correct.
- **C19.21 (rejected):** VReg-capacity overflow is not fail closed. `find_or_create_vreg()` returns `NULL` after 128 VRegs, but `tu_live_analyze()` ignores that result and returns success, potentially constructing a truncated value set.
- **C19.22 (qualified):** Interference is built independently per W/A/O region from inclusive interval overlap among non-implicit definitions. This is a deterministic graph over the allocator’s constructed VRegs, not proof that source byte ranges or uses were assigned to the right values.
- **C19.23 (blocked):** Interference-matrix `calloc` failure has no checked error path in the void builder. Safe deterministic allocation-failure injection must be designed before making an executable failure-atomicity claim; otherwise retain this as structural evidence.

## Capacity, placement, and spilling

- **C19.24 (rejected):** Effective region capacity is computed as unsigned `region_capacity - safety_margin` without first proving margin does not exceed capacity. A too-large margin can underflow into a huge apparent capacity.
- **C19.25 (qualified):** Placement is greedy in first-definition order. BEST_FIT uses a four-byte step and then a bytewise retry when spilling is enabled; FIRST_FIT and WORST_FIT both appear to use the same ascending sixteen-byte search. The three enum names do not establish three distinct fragmentation policies.
- **C19.26 (rejected):** Capacity failure with spilling disabled does not reject the program. The allocator force-places the unplaced VReg at offset zero and continues, allowing interfering values to alias while later output can still report valid.
- **C19.27 (rejected):** Spill-victim selection skips VRegs already assigned a physical offset, so it does not evict an occupied interfering value to make room. Marking an unplaced candidate spilled does not free an existing placement; if the current VReg still cannot fit, it is marked spilled too.
- **C19.28 (qualified):** `num_spills` and `spill_bytes` count VRegs marked spilled, not verified backing-store transfers or successful capacity relief. Spill slots are declared but no allocator path assigns a distinct backing address or uses `TU_LIVE_MAX_SPILL_SLOTS` to manage one.
- **C19.29 (rejected):** Spilled VRegs can retain `physical_offset=UINT32_MAX`. Synthetic fill/store constructors truncate SRAM offset and size into 16-bit instruction fields, so unassigned offsets can become `0xffff` and sizes above 65,535 bytes can silently truncate.
- **C19.30 (rejected):** Synthetic spill/fill placement does not implement a conventional preserve-and-reload contract. The apply loop emits a fill before every instruction strictly after the definition through `last_use`, regardless of whether that instruction reads the VReg, retains the original defining instruction, and emits a store only after the last use. No DRAM/backing pointer or spill-slot identity is encoded.

## Operand rewriting and output closure

- **C19.31 (rejected):** Operand rewriting is partial. It patches selected DMA loads, selected O-producing forms, and selected MMA fields; recognized definition/use extraction covers additional opcodes that patching does not. “Allocation succeeds” does not mean every supported operand was rewritten.
- **C19.32 (rejected):** MMA read rewriting can bind the wrong physical value. The patch loop overwrites W and A operands for every VReg whose interval contains the instruction, without checking the original virtual byte range; later matching-region VRegs win by iteration order.
- **C19.33 (rejected):** Output closure is not fail closed. The apply loop stops when the fixed `2 * TU_SCHED_MAX_INSTRS` output capacity is reached, can omit an input suffix or synthetic records, then unconditionally sets `output.valid=true` and returns success.
- **C19.34 (qualified):** Peak W/A/O usage fields are source-local high-water summaries, not exact simultaneous occupancy. W usage is never reclaimed; A/O usage resets to zero when any corresponding non-spilled VReg dies. A peak below nominal capacity does not prove non-overlapping legal placement.
- **C19.35 (rejected):** The allocator does not currently provide executable semantic-equivalence evidence. Focused tests primarily assert return success, `valid`, presence, nonzero usage, or loose bounds; they do not execute original and transformed programs against an independent oracle and compare effects.

## Documentation, Chapter 11 boundary, and safe interpretation

- **C19.36 (qualified):** `docs/compiler-scheduling-pass.md` and `docs/liveness-allocation.md` describe intended pipelines and show illustrative `tu_core.c` integration snippets. Whole-tree caller inventory disproves those snippets as current executable reachability. Their numerical utilization/overlap claims lack retained calibration at the pin and are historical/design-intent evidence.
- **C19.37 (verified):** Chapter 11 remains authoritative for expanded-ISA metadata, text ASM, command-queue admission/readiness/dispatch/completion/signaling/barrier/reset/reclamation, and the scheduler findings already sealed there. Chapter 19 imports those results but owns cross-pass operand interpretation, allocator semantics, and final static rewrite legality.
- **C19.38 (rejected):** A synthetic `DMA_LOAD`/`DMA_STORE` opcode inserted by liveness does not establish Chapter 10 descriptor ownership, transfer execution, dependency readiness, backing-store bytes, completion, or reclamation. Shared DMA naming is not a transport bridge.
- **C19.39 (qualified):** Material architecture alternatives remain regime-specific: conservative order/range analysis can reduce optimization risk but increase serialization and SRAM pressure; more precise range/value analysis can improve reuse but increases compiler state and proof burden; no-spill rejection, explicit backing-store spill, live-range splitting, and larger scratchpads trade traffic, capacity, control complexity, area/power, and verification cost differently. The pin does not establish one universally best policy.
- **C19.40 (blocked):** End-to-end transformed-sequence correctness is blocked outside a bounded executable opcode subset until a repository-contained consumer or independent semantic oracle can interpret both original and rewritten instructions with explicit host/SRAM/backing-store ownership. Structural legality can still be audited fail closed without reviving the broken compiler/runtime narrative.
- **C19.41 (rejected):** Liveness opcode classification is not a closed recognized subset. The unbounded `opcode >= TU_ISA_LAYER_NORM` predicate catches numerically later pooling, DMA, layout, sparsity, and configuration opcodes; some later specialized branches are unreachable. An exhaustive opcode-access census is required before any per-op legality statement.
- **C19.42 (rejected):** Scheduler and liveness disagree on strided-DMA region decoding: scheduler uses `flags[3:2]`, while liveness uses `flags[1:0]`. The same instruction can therefore name different W/A/O regions in the adjacent passes.
- **C19.43 (blocked):** Low-level result provenance is unchecked. `tu_live_apply()` accepts an analysis result and instruction array without proving they belong together, while scheduler validation accepts a caller-provided result/graph pair. Cross-sequence misuse must be probed; successful return alone does not authorize a transform.
- **C19.44 (rejected):** Maximum-dimension MMA range arithmetic has signed-overflow undefined behavior before assignment to wider unsigned fields because promoted 16-bit dimensions are multiplied as `int`. UBSan reaches this defect in scheduler and liveness source. Other range-end and placement additions remain bounded-audit obligations.
- **C19.45 (rejected):** Rebuilding interference on one result is not idempotent: graph lists are not reset and prior matrices are not freed, so repeated calls duplicate nodes and can synthesize false interference. The public low-level API exposes neither an ownership contract nor a destructor.
- **C19.46 (qualified):** Liveness `verbose` is declared/defaulted but inert, while invalid scheduler policy and liveness placement/spill enum values lack explicit rejection. These controls need individual executable dispositions.
- **C19.47 (qualified):** The 128-VReg table is global across W/A/O even though header wording can be read as per-region. The implemented global bound and fail-open overflow behavior are separate claims.
- **C19.48 (qualified):** Makefile test recipes link with `-L. -ltucmodel`, which may select a pre-existing shared object before the archive. Canonical evidence must link the exact archive path directly or prove the shared object absent; target success alone does not seal object provenance.
- **C19.49 (rejected):** Scheduler operand meaning is not a shared ISA contract. DMA offset/channel and MMA range interpretations conflict with adjacent ISA documentation and global flag decoding; the probe shows `TU_FLAG_PREC_FP32` selecting A-SRAM and zero DMA size becoming one byte.
- **C19.50 (rejected):** Hoist reporting chooses the minimum predecessor ID although its comment claims the latest predecessor. A two-predecessor probe reports a move target before a later predecessor; the count is not evidence of legal movement even before considering that no movement occurs.
- **C19.51 (rejected):** Barrier reporting recognizes a narrow DMA-store-to-later-compute successor pattern, generally a WAR relation, but not the common earlier compute write followed by DMA-store read. Documentation and focused test names overstate this asymmetric count-only behavior.
- **C19.52 (qualified):** Scheduler opcode classification and range extraction are internally inconsistent. The access census finds `ELEMENTWISE`/`SCALE` mapped to generic O read/write while `GROUP_NORM`, `DECOMPRESS`, and `COMPRESS` produce no access effects; relevant specialized branches are unreachable or bypassed.
- **C19.53 (rejected):** Invalid scheduler policy values are not rejected and silently take the BALANCED default branch. Likewise, `max_window` does not constrain or partition scheduling.
- **C19.54 (rejected):** Sixteen-entry successor saturation can remove the reciprocal predecessor edge from a seventeenth consumer. A bounded probe constructs one low-priority producer and seventeen disjoint high-priority consumers; the untracked seventeenth consumer is emitted before its producer while DAG construction and result validity succeed.
- **C19.55 (rejected):** Scheduler validation can accept a completely unrelated same-length result with `valid=true`: unmatched graph nodes are skipped, count equality is not a bijection, and graph provenance/built state is unchecked.
- **C19.56 (qualified):** The scheduler sweep is a report, not a fail-closed test gate: it omits exact orders, ignores per-run failures, and returns success from `main`. Focused tests also contain presence-only and nonnegative assertions. Canonical evidence must retain exact probe output and independent failure-sensitive mutations.
